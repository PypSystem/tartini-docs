The base has no color: light paper and a warm ink, #14120F, that is never pure black. On top of it come two signals, terracotta for people and cobalt for artificial intelligence, and nothing else.

---

## The principle

Cobalt is the most protected color in the system. When it starts being used as a link color or as a highlight, the customer loses the only reliable cue for knowing they are reading a machine.

Green, red, and amber appear by meaning: green for done, connected, and healthy; red for real danger, such as deleting, logging out, validation errors, and a channel that is down; amber for a pending state that is not yet an error. Using red as a highlight spends the only word the system has for saying danger.

SAN blue, #007BFF, stays out of the product interface: it collides with cobalt. Its place is the provenance seal.

---

## Ratio

The screen is almost entirely neutral: about 60% paper, 25% ink, with the rest split between the two signals. A screen that is mostly terracotta or mostly cobalt is wrong even if it uses the right colors.
